# What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the branch of Computer Science that focuses on building systems capable of performing tasks that normally require human intelligence.

Examples include:

* Learning
* Reasoning
* Planning
* Problem solving
* Vision
* Speech recognition
* Natural language understanding
* Decision making

---

## Simple Definition (Exam)

> Artificial Intelligence is the science and engineering of creating intelligent machines capable of perceiving, reasoning, learning, and acting to achieve goals.

---

# Why Do We Need AI?

Traditional software follows predefined rules.

```
Input
   ↓
Program
   ↓
Output
```

AI systems instead:

```
Input
   ↓
Learning
   ↓
Knowledge
   ↓
Decision
   ↓
Action
```

They improve with experience rather than relying solely on hard-coded rules.

# Why AI is Difficult

Humans perform many tasks effortlessly that are extremely difficult for computers.

Examples:

* Recognizing faces
* Understanding sarcasm
* Driving in traffic
* Understanding natural language
* Common-sense reasoning
* Learning from few examples

# Four Ways to Define AI

One of the most important topics in Chapter 1 is the four classical approaches to defining AI.

| Thinking          | Acting          |
| ----------------- | --------------- |
| Think Like Humans | Act Like Humans |
| Think Rationally  | Act Rationally  |

These four perspectives have shaped AI research over time.

---

## 1. Thinking Like Humans

### Goal

Create machines that think as humans do.

This approach studies:

* Psychology
* Neuroscience
* Cognitive Science

Example:

Trying to design a computer that solves problems the same way a human would.

### Advantages

* Mimics human intelligence.
* Helps understand the human brain.

### Disadvantages

* Human thinking is difficult to observe directly.
* Different people think differently.
* Not always optimal.

---

## 2. Acting Like Humans

### Goal

Machines should behave like humans.

The classic benchmark is the **Turing Test**, which evaluates whether a machine's behavior is indistinguishable from a human's.

Requirements include:

* Natural language processing
* Knowledge representation
* Automated reasoning
* Machine learning

---

## 3. Thinking Rationally

### Goal

Machines should think according to the rules of logic.

Example:

If

```
All humans are mortal
Socrates is a human
```

Then

```
Socrates is mortal
```

Advantages:

* Logical
* Consistent

Limitations:

* Real-world information is often incomplete or uncertain.
* Logic alone cannot solve every problem.

---

## 4. Acting Rationally (Modern AI)

This is the dominant approach in modern AI and underpins the rest of the book. AI systems are viewed as **rational agents** that perceive their environment and choose actions expected to achieve the best outcomes. 

A rational agent:

* Observes its environment
* Makes decisions
* Maximizes expected performance
* Learns from experience

Example:

A self-driving car:

* Detects traffic
* Predicts other vehicles' behavior
* Plans a route
* Drives safely toward its destination

It does **not** need to think exactly like a human—it only needs to act effectively.

---

# Comparison of the Four Approaches

| Approach          | Focus               | Example                     |
| ----------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Think Like Humans | Cognitive processes | Simulate the human brain    |
| Act Like Humans   | Human behavior      | Pass the Turing Test        |
| Think Rationally  | Logical reasoning   | Prove mathematical theorems |
| Act Rationally    | Optimal actions     | Autonomous vehicles         |

# Key Exam Definitions

### Artificial Intelligence

The science and engineering of building intelligent machines.

### Intelligence

The ability to perceive, learn, reason, solve problems, and adapt to achieve goals.

### Rationality

Choosing the action that is expected to produce the best outcome based on available information.
